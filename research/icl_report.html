SmolVLA z optimization on hard inits: VLM in context vs CEM (LIBERO-Object)

Follow-up to steering round 2. For one hard init state per task, how many candidate z does an optimizer need before the policy solves that init? Optimizers are compared on identical seeds: a VLM that proposes z in context (as is, and with its coordinates randomly permuted per chain), the same model without images (text only), and CEM. The best z of every chain is then re-evaluated on 32 held-out noise seeds.

Setup

  • Policy HuggingFaceVLA/smolvla_libero, suite libero_object. Steering as in round 2: FiLM on the action-expert MLP outputs of layers 7, 15, 23, 31, [γ;β] = α·W·z with random W (seed 0), α = 0.3, z ∈ ℝ⁸. Coordinates are limited to [−1.5, 1.5] on a 0.1 grid.
  • Tasks and inits: t0 init 4, t7 init 6, t9 init 3, t4 init 7 (inits with low z=0 success).
  • Chain = one (init, repeat) optimization run. Candidate = one z evaluated in a chain, on the same K = 8 flow-noise seeds of that chain. Rate = successes / 8; ties are ranked by the mean steps of the successful rollouts (episode limit 280). Candidate 0 is always z = 0 (the unmodified policy). Budget: 30 candidates per chain, including z = 0; a chain stops early when a candidate reaches 8/8.
  • VLM (qwen38-27b, temperature 0.3): one z per round. The prompt holds the task text, all evaluated candidates with their results, and two rollout images of the last candidate (5 frames, third-person | wrist). Up to 3 attempts per round (higher temperature on retries); if all fail, a random z is used (fallback_random). VLM-PERM: same VLM, but the policy receives zpolicy = M·z with a random signed permutation M per chain (logged as z_policy). Added after the first VLM chains showed a fixed habit (probe +e₀, then −e₀, then e₁ …): without M every repeat probes the same policy directions in the same order, so repeats are not independent and the result depends on which directions of W happen to come first.
    CEM: population 6, 2 elites, σ₀ = 0.5, 5 generations; generation 0 contains z = 0; per-coordinate σ is updated as ½σ + ½·std(elites), floor 0.1. CEM is invariant to permuting coordinates, so it needs no PERM variant.
    LLM-PERM (round 2, local): VLM-PERM with the images removed; the prompt keeps the task text and the full history (z, successes, mean steps). Same model, temperature, permutations M and seeds. t7 and t9 only, r0–r4.
  • Repeats r0–r2 per task and method (Sol); local extra repeats r3–r4: t0 (VLM, CEM), t9 and t7 (VLM-PERM, CEM). The same (init, repeat) uses the same noise seeds for all methods, so chains are paired. Different repeats use different seeds, which is why the z = 0 rate of one init varies between repeats (e.g. t4 init 7: 0, 3, 6 of 8).
    Results are bit-identical only on the same machine. The same z and seeds give different rollouts on the local RTX 4090 and on a Sol A100 (t7 init 6, z = 0: r0 1/8 vs 2/8, r1 3/8 vs 1/8). Local LLM-PERM r0–r2 share seeds with the Sol VLM-PERM/CEM chains but are not strictly paired. Strict pairs are the local r3–r4.
  • Held-out re-evaluation (round 2, local): the best candidate of every finished chain (highest rate, ties by steps), plus z = 0, on 32 new noise seeds per init (seed + init + 1000·(10000 + j)). The seeds are shared by all chains of an init.
  • Main metric: sample efficiency, the first candidate index at which a chain reaches ≥ 6/8 or 8/8.
  • Code: research/{optimize,heldout,plot_icl}.py, jobs from experiments/libero/icl/lines/ (Sol) and experiments/libero/icl/local_round2.sh (LLM-PERM + held-out). Raw data: out/libero/smolvla/icl/hpc/ (local copy of Sol results/smolvla/icl/) and out/icl-local/, held-out in out/icl-heldout/.

Takeaways

  • z is a usable test-time adaptation space for single hard inits, and the gain holds on new seeds. On 32 held-out seeds, the best z of a chain succeeds 0.78–0.82 on average (VLM, VLM-PERM, CEM) vs z = 0 at 0.34 / 0.12 / 0.00 / 0.31 (t0 / t7 / t9 / t4). 46 of 48 chains beat z = 0. On t9, z = 0 solves 0 of 32 held-out seeds, while the best z average 0.75–0.81.
  • Selection optimism is moderate. z that reached 8/8 on their 8 seeds keep about 0.89 on held-out seeds (all methods alike). t4 drops the most (e.g. VLM-PERM r0: 7/8 → 0.41), so 8 seeds are least reliable there.
  • VLM-PERM is faster to find a good z, but not better at the end. To reach 8/8, VLM-PERM needed a median of 9.5 candidates vs 18.5 for CEM (paired by init and repeat: 9 wins, 5 losses, 2 ties; Wilcoxon p = 0.10, n = 16). Held-out quality of the final z is the same (0.82 vs 0.81). The advantage is sample efficiency, not a better optimum.
  • Images seem to help, mostly on t7 (weak evidence). Text-only LLM-PERM reached 8/8 in 2/5 t7 chains (VLM-PERM 5/5), and three t7 chains stayed at ≤ 3/8 after 30 candidates. On t9 it matched VLM-PERM (4/5 vs 3/5, median 15 vs 11). Over the 10 t7 + t9 pairs: first 8/8 mean 20.2 vs 15.6 candidates (VLM 6 wins, LLM 2, 2 ties; Wilcoxon p = 0.30); held-out rate of the best z 0.65 vs 0.83 (p = 0.22). Caveat: only r3–r4 are same-machine pairs (VLM 2 wins, LLM 1, 1 tie).
  • Without the permutation the VLM result depends on which coordinate it probes first. The VLM always probed +e₀, then −e₀, then e₁ … On t0, −e₀ happens to be a good direction, and all 5 VLM chains reached 8/8 at candidates 3–5. On t9 only 1/3 did. Per-chain random signed permutations remove this shared bias; VLM-PERM is the fair VLM arm.
  • VLM cost and reliability: 591 VLM/LLM proposals, 583 accepted on the first attempt, 1 random fallback. Median latency 70 s (VLM-PERM) and 63 s (LLM-PERM), about 2k completion tokens per call in both. The text-only arm is barely cheaper because the reasoning tokens dominate the cost.
  • Caveats. One W, one init per task, n = 3–5 chains per task and method, one model. The LLM-PERM comparison covers only t7 and t9 and is mostly cross-machine. The rollouts are chaotic: the same seeds give different outcomes on different GPUs.
