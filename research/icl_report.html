SmolVLA z optimization on hard inits: VLM in context vs CEM (LIBERO-Object)

Follow-up to steering round 2. For one hard init state per task, how many candidate z does an optimizer need before the policy solves that init? Optimizers are compared on identical seeds: a VLM that proposes z in context (as is, and with its coordinates randomly permuted per chain), and CEM.

Setup

  • Policy HuggingFaceVLA/smolvla_libero, suite libero_object. Steering as in round 2: FiLM on the action-expert MLP outputs of layers 7, 15, 23, 31, [γ;β] = α·W·z with random W (seed 0), α = 0.3, z ∈ ℝ⁸. Coordinates are limited to [−1.5, 1.5] on a 0.1 grid.
  • Tasks and inits: t0 init 4, t7 init 6, t9 init 3, t4 init 7 (inits with low z=0 success).
  • Chain = one (init, repeat) optimization run. Candidate = one z evaluated in a chain, on the same K = 8 flow-noise seeds of that chain. Rate = successes / 8; ties are ranked by the mean steps of the successful rollouts (episode limit 280). Candidate 0 is always z = 0 (the unmodified policy). Budget: 30 candidates per chain, including z = 0; a chain stops early when a candidate reaches 8/8.
  • VLM (qwen38-27b, temperature 0.3): one z per round. The prompt holds the task text, all evaluated candidates with their results, and two rollout images of the last candidate (5 frames, third-person | wrist). Up to 3 attempts per round (higher temperature on retries); if all fail, a random z is used (fallback_random). VLM-PERM: same VLM, but the policy receives zpolicy = M·z with a random signed permutation M per chain (logged as z_policy). Added after the first VLM chains showed a fixed habit (probe +e₀, then −e₀, then e₁ …): without M every repeat probes the same policy directions in the same order, so repeats are not independent and the result depends on which directions of W happen to come first.
    CEM: population 6, 2 elites, σ₀ = 0.5, 5 generations; generation 0 contains z = 0; per-coordinate σ is updated as ½σ + ½·std(elites), floor 0.1. CEM is invariant to permuting coordinates, so it needs no PERM variant.
  • Repeats r0–r2 per task and method (Sol); local extra repeats r3–r4: t0 (VLM, CEM), t9 and t7 (VLM-PERM, CEM). The same (init, repeat) uses the same noise seeds for all methods, so chains are paired. Different repeats use different seeds, which is why the z = 0 rate of one init varies between repeats (e.g. t4 init 7: 0, 3, 6 of 8).
  • Main metric: sample efficiency, the first candidate index at which a chain reaches ≥ 6/8 or 8/8.
  • Code: research/{optimize,plot_icl}.py, jobs from experiments/libero/icl/lines/. Raw data: out/libero/smolvla/icl/hpc/ (local copy of Sol results/smolvla/icl/) and out/icl-local/.

Takeaways

  • z is a usable test-time adaptation space for single hard inits. Starting from inits where z = 0 succeeds 0–2 of 8 (most repeats), a z with 8/8 on the chain's seeds was found within 30 candidates in 13/16 VLM-PERM, 13/18 CEM and 10/14 VLM chains. The best z stay moderate (median ‖z‖ ≈ 1.7–1.9, inside the usable range of round 2).
  • ≥ 6/8 is easy, 8/8 separates the methods. CEM's random generation 0 often reaches ≥ 6/8 within 1–3 candidates (pooled median 5.5 vs 6 for VLM-PERM), so good z are common near the origin. To reach 8/8, VLM-PERM needed a median of 9.5 candidates vs 18.5 for CEM (paired by init and repeat: 9 wins, 5 losses, 2 ties; mean 13.6 vs 18.7, chains that never reached 8/8 counted as 30; Wilcoxon p = 0.10, n = 16). Suggestive, not significant.
  • Without the permutation the VLM result depends on which coordinate it probes first. The VLM always probed +e₀, then −e₀, then e₁ … On t0, −e₀ happens to be a good direction, and all 5 VLM chains reached 8/8 at candidates 3–5. On t9 only 1/3 did. Paired against CEM, the plain VLM is even (5 wins, 6 losses, 3 ties). Per-chain random signed permutations remove this shared bias; VLM-PERM is the fair VLM arm.
  • Hardest init (t9 init 3, z = 0 at 0–1/8): 8/8 in 3/5 VLM-PERM chains (at candidates 8–11) vs 2/5 CEM chains (18, 21).
  • VLM cost and reliability: 393 proposals: 388 accepted on the first attempt, 4 after one retry, 1 random fallback. Median latency 70 s (p90 142 s, 6 chains calling in parallel), about 2k completion tokens per call. On a shared A100 a VLM round is about 5 min of rollouts plus about 1 min of VLM, and the VLM proposes one candidate per round while CEM evaluates 6 per batch. So VLM-PERM uses fewer rollouts but takes more wall-clock time per candidate.
  • Caveats. Rates are measured on the same 8 seeds used for selection; no held-out re-evaluation this round, so "8/8" means solved for those seeds, with best-of-N optimism affecting all methods. One W, one init per task, n = 3–5 chains per task and method, one VLM model, and no text-only ablation (the effect of the images is untested).
