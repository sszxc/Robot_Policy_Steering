SmolVLA z → FiLM steering, round 2 (LIBERO-Object, 5 tasks)

Repeat of the round-1 test with the fixes found in its review: orthonormal, equal-norm probe directions; three seeds of the random W; PCA fitted on inits that are never evaluated; five tasks; grid and line scans on every task; held-out tests that treat the two noise reps of an init as one unit and correct for multiple picks. Cross-task transfer was not run this round.

Short answer.

  • The round-1 sign effect (+u beating −u on all 8 directions) is gone with orthogonal directions: across 20 grids, +c beats −c in 164 (direction, |c|) cells and −c beats +c in 200 (116 ties; sign test p = 0.07). It came from the correlated u_k, not the policy.
  • Steering gains replicate on unseen inits for every W: the top grid pick lifts t0 from 0.38 to 0.82–0.92 and t7 from 0.42 to 0.80–0.90 (all eight Holm p ≤ 0.002). On t9 / t4 (z=0 already 0.88 / 0.78) picks reach 0.93–0.97 / 0.90–0.93, with no losses on t9, but none survive multiple-testing correction at n = 30 inits. t2 is at the ceiling.
  • All three random-W seeds and the PCA W reach the same level, so this is not a lucky W.
  • Caveat that remains: on the two low tasks, the average random direction at |c| = 0.5 already beats z=0 (t0 0.39–0.58 vs 0.34; t7 0.47–0.73 vs 0.44). Part of the gain may be "any small perturbation breaks the empty-grasp failure", not a specific direction. A matched-norm random-z control on held-out inits is the next check.

Setup

  • Policy HuggingFaceVLA/smolvla_libero, suite libero_object, tasks t0, t7, t9, t4, t2 (round-1 laptop scan 3, 3, 5, 6, 8 / 8: two low, two mid, one ceiling). Replans every step; flow-matching noise paired by (init, rep).
  • Injection unchanged: FiLM on action-expert MLP outputs of layers 7, 15, 23, 31, [γ;β] = α·W·z, z ∈ ℝ⁸, same for all denoising steps. random W (γ and β, α = 0.3) with seeds 0, 1, 2 (random-s0..2); pca W (β only, top-8 PCs × √λ, α = 3.0) fitted per task on inits 40–49 × 3 noise reps (30 unsteered episodes).
  • Directions: u_0…u_7 are an orthonormal basis of ℝ⁸ (random rotation, z_seed 0) scaled to ‖u_k‖ = √8, the same for every task and W. Test points z = c·u_k, so |z| = c·√8 for every direction. With this norm, scale c = 1 gives median open-loop |Δa₀| ≈ 0.10–0.13 for all four W (figure below), i.e. the round-1 α calibration still holds.
  • Grid: c ∈ {±0.5, ±1, ±2} × 8 directions × inits 0–7 (1 noise rep) per W and task; z=0 on inits 0–7 × 8 noise reps. Line scans (random-s0 and pca): the best grid direction and one reference direction at c ∈ {±0.25, ±0.75, ±1.5, ±3}, merged with the grid points of the same ray (same inits and noise; z=0 reruns are bit-identical). Held-out: the top grid pick per (task, W) on inits 10–39 × 2 noise reps (n = 60), paired with z=0.
  • Selection rule (research/select_z.py): highest grid success, ties broken by the mean success of the neighboring scales on the same ray.
  • Code: research/{run,fit_pca,select_z,openloop,scene,plot_steer2}.py, jobs from experiments/libero/steer2/make_lines.py via platforms/hpc/slurm/steer_lines.sbatch. Raw data: out/libero/smolvla/steer2/hpc/ (local copy), Sol results/smolvla/steer2/.

Do directions mean the same thing on different tasks? For random W (the same W and u_k for all tasks), the per-(direction, c) success at |c| ≤ 1 correlates across t0/t7/t9/t4 with mean r = 0.59 (s0), 0.72 (s1), 0.64 (s2). For PCA W, which is fitted separately per task, r = 0.14. With a fixed W, a z direction has a largely task-general effect. Example: for random-s0, u5 is the held-out pick on t0, t7, t9 and t4.

Trajectory effect and consistency

This weakens a round-1 statement. On t2 (unimodal, 6 cm noise) the effect exceeds noise from |c| = 1 (9–18 cm) and is strongly consistent at |c| = 2 (main-effect fraction 0.6–0.9). On the low tasks the noise reference is itself large (t0 26 cm, t7 24 cm), because z=0 reps already split into success and failure paths. There, at |c| ≤ 1 the path change is about the same size as the noise change: steering acts by tipping between the same modes the noise already visits, not by producing new paths. Overdispersion of per-direction success (variance over binomial) is 1–6 at |c| = 1–2 on t0/t7/t4/t2 but mostly ≈ 1 on t9 at |c| ≤ 1, so directions differ more than chance where there is room to move.

Line scans

All curves are smooth and roughly unimodal; nearly all fail at |c| = 3 (exceptions: t0 PCA u3 still 0.63 at c = −3, t9 PCA u0 0.25). Random-W rays have a plateau that is usually off-center (for example, t0 / t7 u5 peak at c = +1 and fall off on the negative side). PCA rays are more sign-odd: t0 u3, t7 u2 and t4 u5 succeed on one side up to c = −1…−2 and fail on the other by c = +0.5…+0.75. That matches round 1: PCA directions act more like one-sided knobs, random directions like local bumps.

Held-out validation

Table: one pick per (task, W). Wins / losses count (init, rep) pairs where only the pick or only z=0 succeeded. "init-level p" is a Wilcoxon test on the per-init mean difference (the two noise reps of an init are not independent; the sign test treats them as independent and is too optimistic). Holm corrects the init-level p over all 20 picks. Δsteps: mean change of episode length on pairs where both succeed.

  • Low tasks: every pick replicates (t0 27–35 wins vs 0–5 losses; t7 23–29 vs 0–2). The winner's curse is modest: grid 0.88–1.0 in-sample vs 0.80–0.92 held-out.
  • Mid tasks: the direction of the effect holds (t9: 17 wins, 0 losses pooled over 4 picks; t4: 40 vs 7), but each single pick has too few discordant pairs at n = 30 inits to pass Holm. A pooled or larger test would settle it.
  • Ceiling t2: no success change possible; picks change speed instead (below).

What the picks change

Share of held-out episodes in which the gripper closes on nothing (finger width < 1 cm) at least once. At z=0 this happens in 65% / 60% of t0 / t7 episodes and in 86–100% of their failures; with the picks it drops to 7–27%. The gains come from fixing the missed grasp, the same mechanism as in round 1.

Speed on episodes where both succeed: t4 random-s0/s1 picks are 11–12 steps faster (p ≤ 5e-5); t2 random picks 4–6 steps faster (p ≤ 5e-5) while PCA u3 c = +0.5 is 4 steps slower (p = 7e-6); t9 random-s2 / PCA picks are 18 / 5 steps slower (p ≤ 2e-5). So z also moves episode speed at unchanged success, in either direction depending on the pick.

Takeaways

  • Controllability. Directions in z differ reliably in effect. With a fixed random W their effect is largely shared across tasks (r ≈ 0.6–0.7), which is what a reusable control space needs. Effects are smooth along rays.
  • Budget. The usable region is |c| ≲ 1 (|z| ≲ 2.8, open-loop |Δa₀| ≲ 0.1–0.13, about ⅓ of the flow-noise spread); c = 2 breaks most tasks, c = 3 all. A norm bound or open-loop |Δa| budget is needed for any optimizer.
  • Optimizability. 48 candidates × 8 inits already find held-out gains on every W and every task with headroom. Random-W rays have wider plateaus (easier for local search). PCA rays are more one-sided (more interpretable knobs). Random W is not worse than PCA W, so a fitted basis is not required.
  • Open question before CEM. How much of the low-task gain is direction-specific? Test: held-out success of K random z at the same |z| as the pick (matched-norm control) against the pick and z=0.

Caveats

  • One U (z_seed 0) shared by all W. For random W the rotation does not matter statistically; for PCA, u_k mix PCs (they are not single PCs).
  • Grid picks are scored on 8 inits × 1 noise rep. Mid tasks would need about 2× more held-out inits for single-pick significance.
  • The base policy is below published SmolVLA LIBERO numbers in this pipeline (round-1 scan 63/80 on Object). Gains may partly compensate for an evaluation mismatch; this is not checked.
